Explanation A.4.3.26.1.

Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem.
Assume \(387\) is \(x\%\) of \(430\text{,}\) so “\(x\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(387\) out of \(430\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{x}{100} \amp = \frac{387}{430} \\ 430x \amp = 387 \cdot 100 \\ 430x \amp = 38700 \\ \frac{430x}{430} \amp = \frac{38700}{430} \\ x \amp = 90 \end{aligned} }\)
\(387\) is \(90\%\) of \(430\text{.}\)
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem.
This translation from English to may should help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \Longleftrightarrow 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
Assume \(387\) is \(x\) (as a percent) of \(430\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 387 \amp = x \cdot 430 \\ \frac{387}{430} \amp = \frac{x \cdot 430}{430} \\ 0.9 \amp = x\\ x \amp = 90\% \end{aligned} }\)
\(387\) is \(90\%\) of \(430\text{.}\)
Method 3
In the sentence “\(387\) is what percent of \(430\text{,}\)”
  • \(387\) is the percentage,
  • “what percent” is the rate,
  • \(430\) is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{rate} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{base}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{rate} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{base}} = \frac{387}{430} = 0.9 = 90\% }\)
\(387\) is \(90\%\) of \(430\text{.}\)
