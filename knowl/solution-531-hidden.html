When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 8x-10y=0 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 8x-10y \amp = 0 \\ 8x-10y\mathbf{{}-8x} \amp = 0\mathbf{{}-8x} \\ -10y \amp = -8x \\ \frac{-10y}{-10} \amp = \frac{-8x}{-10} \\ y \amp = \frac{8}{10}x \\ y \amp = \frac{4}{5}x \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(\displaystyle{ \frac{4}{5} }\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \({\left(0,0\right)}\text{.}\)
