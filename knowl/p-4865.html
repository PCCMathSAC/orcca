In further rows, notice that as row-to-row change in \(x\) increases, row-to-row change in \(y\) increases proportionally to preserve the constant rate of change. Looking at the change in the last two rows of the table, we see \(x\) increases by \(5\) and \(y\) increases by \(100\text{,}\) which gives a rate of change of \(\frac{100\,\text{dollars}}{5\,\text{wk}} = 20\,\frac{\text{dollars}}{\text{wk}}\text{,}\) the value of the slope again.
