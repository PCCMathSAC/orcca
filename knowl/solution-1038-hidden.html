This problem can be boiled down to this question: What is \(20\%\) of \(43\text{?}\) We will show multiple methods to solve this problem.
Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem. Assume \(20\%\) of \(43\) is \(x\text{,}\) so “\(20\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(x\) out of \(43\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{20}{100} \amp = \frac{x}{43} \\ 100x \amp = 20 \cdot 43 \\ 100x \amp = 860 \\ \frac{100x}{100} \amp = \frac{860}{100} \\ x \amp = 8.6 \end{aligned} }\)
Emily will pay \({\$8.60}\) in tip.
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem. This translation from English to math may help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \iff 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
The question is: What is \(20\%\) of \(43\text{?}\) Assume \(x\) is \(20\%\) of \(43\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} x \amp = 0.2 \cdot 43 \\ \amp = 8.6 \end{aligned} }\)
Emily will pay \({\$8.60}\) in tip.
Method 3
In the sentence “What is \(20\%\) of \(43\text{,}\)”
  • “what” is the percentage,
  • \(20\%\) is the rate,
  • \(43\) is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{percentage} = \text{rate} \cdot \text{base}\text{,}\) we do a multiplication to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{percentage } = \text{rate} \cdot \text{base} = 20\% \cdot 43 = 0.2 \cdot 43 = 8.6 }\)
Emily will pay \({\$8.60}\) in tip.
