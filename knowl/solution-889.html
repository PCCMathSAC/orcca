Explanation A.2.7.48.1.

To add two fractions with different denominators, we first have to find a common denominator.
In this case, the least common multiple of \(8\) and \(9\) is \(72\text{.}\)
Look at the first fraction \(\displaystyle{\frac{3}{8}}\text{.}\) To change the denominator to \(72\text{,}\) we multiply \(9\) at both the top and bottom, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{3}{8} \amp = \frac{3 \cdot 9}{8 \cdot 9}\\ \amp = \frac{27}{72}\end{aligned} }\)
Similarly, we change the second fraction’s denominator to \(72\text{:}\)
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{2}{9} \amp = \frac{2 \cdot 8}{9 \cdot 8}\\ \amp = \frac{16}{72} \end{aligned}}\)
Now we can add those two fractions. The full process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{9} \amp = \frac{27}{72} + \frac{16}{72} \\ \amp = \frac{27 + 16}{72} \\ \amp = \frac{43}{72} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer is \(\displaystyle{\frac{43}{72}}\text{.}\)
