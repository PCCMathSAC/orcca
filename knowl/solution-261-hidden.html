We substitute in each ordered pair’s \(x\)- and \(y\)-values into the equation \(y=-9 x - 9\text{,}\) and see whether the resulting equation is true.
In one case, \((-2,12)\text{,}\) we have
\(\begin{aligned} 12 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -9(-2) - 9\\ 12 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 18 - 9\\ 12 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 9\\ \end{aligned}\)
which is a false equation. So \((-2,12)\) is not a solution to the given equation.
In each other case, for example with \((7,-72)\text{,}\) we have a true equation:
\(\begin{aligned} -72 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -9(7) - 9\\ -72 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -63 - 9\\ -72 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -72\\ \end{aligned}\)
So the other three points are solutions.
