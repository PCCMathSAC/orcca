Explanation 3.4.5.3.1.
There are at least two methods to reduce the fraction \(\displaystyle{ \frac{6}{27} }\text{.}\)
Method 1:We find a number that divides into both the numerator \(6\text{,}\) and the denominator \(27\text{.}\)
Check the first few prime numbers one by one: \(2, 3, 5, 7, \ldots\)
In this case, \(3\) divides into both the numerator and denominator of \(\displaystyle{ \frac{6}{27} }\text{.}\) We divide \(3\) into both numbers, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{6}{27} \amp = \frac{6 \div 3}{27\div 3}\\ \amp = \frac{2}{9} \end{aligned}}\)
Next, check again whether any prime number divides into both the numerator and denominator. We need to keep trying until no prime numbers go into both numbers.
In this case, \(\displaystyle{\frac{2}{9}}\) is the final answer.
Method 2:
A second method to reduce fraction is to prime factor both the numerator and the denominator, and then cancel out factors in pairs: one from the numerator and one from the denominator.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{6}{27} \amp = \frac{2 \cdot 3}{3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3} \\ \amp =\frac{2}{3 \cdot 3}\\ \amp = \frac{2}{9} \end{aligned} }\)
