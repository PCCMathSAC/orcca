Assume \(x\) years since its purchase, the car’s value is \(y\) dollars. A linear equation to model the car’s value is:
\(\displaystyle{ y = -2200x+46200 }\)
A line’s \(x\)-intercept looks like \((x,0)\text{.}\) To find its \(x\) intercept, we substitute \(y\) in the equation with \(0\text{,}\) and we have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} y \amp = -2200x+46200 \\ 0 \amp = -2200x+46200 \\ -46200 \amp = -2200x \\ \frac{-46200}{-2200} \amp = \frac{-2200x}{-2200} \\ 21 \amp = x \end{aligned} }\)
The line’s \(x\)-intercept is \((21,0)\text{.}\)
The correct solution is: The x-intercept is (21,0). It implies the car would have no more value 21 years since its purchase.
