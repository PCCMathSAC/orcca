Explanation 2.3.5.46.1.
Let \(h\) be the height of the person in Pamela’s model, which we’ll measure in inches. We’ll create a proportional equation that compares the building and person’s heights in the model to their heights in real life:
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \frac{\text{height of model building in inches}}{\text{height of actual building in feet}}\amp=\frac{\text{height of model person in inches}}{\text{height of actual person in feet}}\\ \frac{7\,\text{in}}{56\,\text{ft}}\amp=\frac{h\,\text{in}}{5\,\text{ft}\,6\text{in}} \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
Before we can just eliminate the units, we’ll need to convert 5 ft 6 in to feet:
\begin{equation*} \frac{2\,\text{in}}{30\,\text{ft}}=\frac{h\,\text{in}}{5.5\,\text{ft}} \end{equation*}
Now we can remove the units and continue solving:
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \frac{7}{56}\amp=\frac{h}{5.5}\\ \multiplyleft{56\cdot5.5}\frac{7}{56}\amp=\frac{h}{5.5}\multiplyright{56\cdot5.5}\\ 5.5\cdot 7 \amp= 56h\\ 38.5 \amp= 56h\\ h\amp\approx 0.6875 \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
Pamela should make the model be \({0.6875\ {\rm in}}\) tall.
