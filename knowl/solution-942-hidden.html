To find the distance between Johnstown and Jerrystown, we need to find the difference between their distance. This implies subtraction. We could treat subtraction as “adding a negative”:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] 4{{\frac{1}{2}}} -1{{\frac{3}{4}}} \amp = 4{{\frac{1}{2}}} + (-1{{\frac{3}{4}}}) \\ \amp = 4 + {{\frac{1}{2}}} + (-1) + (-{{\frac{3}{4}}}) \\ \amp = 4 + (-1) + {{\frac{1}{2}}} + (-{{\frac{3}{4}}}) \\ \amp = 3 + \frac{1 \cdot 2}{2 \cdot 2} + (-\frac{3}{4}) \\ \amp = 3 + \frac{2}{4} + (-\frac{3}{4}) \\ \amp = 3 + \frac{2+(-3)}{4} \\ \amp = 3 + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \end{aligned} }\)
From here, there are two methods to continue.
Method 1
We can “split” \(1\) from the integer, and do \(1 + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}})\text{:}\)
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \amp {\phantom{{}=}} 3 + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \\ \amp = 2 + 1 + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \\ \amp = 2 + \frac{4}{4} + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \\ \amp = 2 + \frac{4-1}{4} \\ \amp = 2 + \frac{3}{4} \\ \amp = {2 {\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}} \end{aligned} }\)
Note that we changed \(1\) to \(\frac{4}{4}\text{.}\)
Method2
We could simply change the whole number \(3\) into a fraction without “splitting” \(1\text{:}\)
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \amp {\phantom{{}=}} 3 + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \\ \amp = \frac{3}{1} + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \\ \amp = \frac{3\cdot4}{1\cdot4} + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \\ \amp = \frac{12}{4} + ({-{\frac{1}{4}}}) \\ \amp = \frac{12+(-1)}{4} \\ \amp = \frac{11}{4} \\ \amp = {2 {\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}} \end{aligned} }\)
