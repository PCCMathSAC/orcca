A line’s slope-intercept equation has the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the \(y\)-coordinate of the \(y\)-intercept. We first find the slope.
To find the slope of a line from its graph, we identify two points, and then draw a slope triangle. It’s wise to choose points with integer coordinates. For this problem, we choose \((0,4)\) and \((4,3)\text{.}\)
Next, we draw a slope triangle and find the "rise" and "run". In this problem, the rise is \(-1\) and the run is \(4\text{.}\)
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned}\text{slope}\amp =\frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}\\\amp =\frac{-1}{4}\\\amp =-{\frac{1}{4}}\end{aligned} \end{equation*}
This line’s slope is \(-{\frac{1}{4}}\text{.}\)
It’s clear in the graph that this line’s \(y\)-intercept is \((0,4)\text{.}\)
So this line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y= -{\frac{1}{4}} x+ 4\text{.}\)
