Explanation 3.10.9.3.1.
We substitute in each ordered pair’s \(x\)- and \(y\)-values into the equation \(y=-\frac{3}{4} x-2\text{,}\) and see whether the resulting equation is true.
In two cases, \((-8,4)\) and \((0,-2)\text{,}\) we have true equations:
\(\begin{aligned} 4 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{3}{4}(-8)-2 \amp -2 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{3}{4}(0)-2\\ 4 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}6-2 \amp -2 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}0-2\\ 4 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}4 \amp -2 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-2\\ \end{aligned}\)
So \((-8,4)\) and \((0,-2)\) are solutions to the given equation.
In the other two cases, \((12,-7)\) and \((-20,18)\text{,}\) we have false equations:
\(\begin{aligned} 18 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{3}{4}(-20)-2 \amp -7 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{3}{4}(12)-2\\ 18 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}15-2 \amp -7 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-9-2\\ 18 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}13 \amp -7 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-11\\ \end{aligned}\)
So \((12,-7)\) and \((-20,18)\) are not solutions.
