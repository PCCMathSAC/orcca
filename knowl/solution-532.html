Explanation 3.7.5.17.1.
When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 10x+8y=5 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 10x+8y \amp = 5 \\ 10x+8y\mathbf{{}-10x} \amp = 5\mathbf{{}-10x} \\ 8y \amp = -10x +5 \\ \frac{8y}{8} \amp = \frac{-10x}{8} + \frac{5}{8}\\ y \amp = -\frac{10}{8}x + \frac{5}{8}\\ y \amp = -\frac{5}{4}x + \frac{5}{8} \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(-\frac{5}{4}\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \(\left(0,\frac{5}{8}\right)\text{.}\)
