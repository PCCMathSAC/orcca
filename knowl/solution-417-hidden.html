The slope-intercept form of a line’s equation has the form \(y=mx+b\) where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the \(y\)-coordinate of the \(y\)-intercept. To find a line’s slope-intercept equation, we first try to find the line’s slope.
To find a line’s slope, we can use the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{slope}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}} }\)
First, we mark which number corresponds to which variable in the formula:
\(\displaystyle{ (1,6) \longrightarrow (x_{1},y_{1}) }\)
\(\displaystyle{ (-3,22) \longrightarrow (x_{2},y_{2}) }\)
Now we substitute these numbers into the corresponding variables in the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\text{slope}\amp =\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\\ \amp =\frac{22-6}{-3-1}\\ \amp =\frac{16}{-4}\\ \amp =-4 \end{aligned}}\)
Now we have \(y=-4x+b\text{.}\) The next step is to find the value of \(b\text{.}\) There are at least two ways we can do this.
One way is to substitute one of the given points into \(y=-4x+b\text{.}\) We choose to use \((1,6)\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y \amp = -4x + b \\ 6 \amp = -4 \cdot 1 + b \\ 6 \amp = -4 + b \\ 6\mathbf{{} + 4} \amp = -4 + b\mathbf{{} + 4} \\ 10 \amp = b\\ b \amp = 10 \end{aligned}\)
This line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y=-4x+10\text{.}\)
Another way is to use the line’s point-slope equation \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\text{.}\) Again, we choose to use the point \((1,6)\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y-y_{1} \amp = m(x-x_{1}) \\ y-6 \amp = -4(x-1) \\ y-6 \amp = -4x - 4 \cdot (-1) \\ y-6 \amp = -4x+4 \\ y-6\mathbf{{}+6} \amp = -4x+4\mathbf{{}+6} \\ y \amp = -4x+10 \end{aligned}\)
This is the second way to find \(b\text{,}\) and thus the line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y=-4x+10\text{.}\)
