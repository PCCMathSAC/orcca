Explanation A.2.7.57.1.

To add two fractions with different denominators, we first have to find a common denominator.
In this case, the least common multiple of \(8\) and \(5\) is \(40\text{.}\)
Look at the first fraction \(\displaystyle{-\frac{1}{8}}\text{.}\) To change the denominator to \(40\text{,}\) we multiply \(5\) at both the top and bottom, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} -\frac{1}{8} \amp = -\frac{1 \cdot 5}{8 \cdot 5}\\ \amp = -\frac{5}{40} \end{aligned}}\)
Similarly, we change the second fraction’s denominator to \(40\text{:}\)
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{2}{5} \amp = \frac{2 \cdot 8}{5 \cdot 8}\\ \amp = \frac{16}{40} \end{aligned}}\)
Now we can add those two fractions. The full process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} -\frac{1}{8} + \frac{2}{5} \amp = -\frac{5}{40} + \frac{16}{40} \\ \amp = \frac{-5 + 16}{40} \\ \amp = \frac{11}{40} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer to this question is \(\displaystyle{\frac{11}{40}}\text{.}\)
