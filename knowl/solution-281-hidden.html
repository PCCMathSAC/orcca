Since this equation has a fractional coefficient for \(x\) with denominator \(2\text{,}\) it would be wise to choose our own \(x\)-values that are multiples of \(2\text{.}\) Then when we use them to solve for \(y\text{,}\) the denominator will be cleared, and we will not need to continue with fraction arithmetic.
This solution will use the \(x\)-values \(-2\text{,}\) \(0\text{,}\) \(2\text{,}\) \(4\) and \(6\text{.}\) The choice to use these \(x\)-values is arbitrary, but they are small multiples of \(2\text{,}\) which will make computation easier.
One at a time, we substitute these \(x\)-values into the equation \(y={\frac{1}{2}}x - 2\text{,}\) and solve for \(y\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} y = {\frac{1}{2}}\left( -2 \right) - 2 \amp \implies y=-3\\ y = {\frac{1}{2}}\left( 0 \right) - 2 \amp \implies y=-2\\ y = {\frac{1}{2}}\left( 2 \right) - 2 \amp \implies y=-1\\ y = {\frac{1}{2}}\left( 4 \right) - 2 \amp \implies y=0\\ y = {\frac{1}{2}}\left( 6 \right) - 2 \amp \implies y=1\\ \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
So the table may be completed as:

\(-2\) \(-3\)
\(0\) \(-2\)
\(2\) \(-1\)
\(4\) \(0\)
\(6\) \(1\)
Values of \(x\) and \(y\) satisfying the equation \(y={\frac{1}{2}}x - 2\)
