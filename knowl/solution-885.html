Explanation A.2.7.44.1.

To add two fractions with different denominators, we first have to find a common denominator.
In this case, the least common multiple of \(10\) and \(20\) is \(20\text{.}\)
Look at the first fraction \(\displaystyle{\frac{1}{10}}\text{.}\) To change the denominator to \(20\text{,}\) we multiply \(2\) at both the top and bottom, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{10} \amp = \frac{1 \cdot 2}{10 \cdot 2}\\ \amp = \frac{2}{20} \end{aligned}}\)
The second fraction already has the common denominator.
Finally, we can do the addition. The whole process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{10} + \frac{7}{20} \amp = \frac{2}{20} + \frac{7}{20} \\ \amp = \frac{2 + 7}{20} \\ \amp = \frac{9}{20} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer is \(\displaystyle{\frac{9}{20}}\text{.}\)
