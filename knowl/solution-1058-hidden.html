This problem can be boiled down to this question: \(16\) is \(20\%\) of what? We will show multiple methods to solve this problem.
Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem. Assume \(16\) is \(20\%\) of \(x\text{,}\) so “\(20\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(16\) out of \(x\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{20}{100} \amp = \frac{16}{x} \\ 20x \amp = 100 \cdot 16 \\ 20x \amp = 1600 \\ \frac{20x}{20} \amp = \frac{1600}{20} \\ x \amp = 80 \end{aligned} }\)
The recommended daily intake of fat for an average person is \({80\ {\rm g}}\text{.}\)
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem. This translation from English to math may help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \iff 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
The question is: \(16\) is \(20\%\) of what? Assume \(16\) is \(20\%\) of \(x\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} 16 \amp = 0.2 \cdot x \\ \frac{16}{0.2} \amp = \frac{0.2x}{0.2} \\ 80 \amp = x \end{aligned} }\)
The recommended daily intake of fat for an average person is \({80\ {\rm g}}\text{.}\)
Method 3
In the sentence “\(16\) is \(20\%\) of what,”
  • \(16\) is the percentage,
  • \(20\%\) is the rate,
  • “what” is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}} = \frac{16}{20\%} = \frac{16}{0.2} = 80 }\)
The recommended daily intake of fat for an average person is \({80\ {\rm g}}\text{.}\)
