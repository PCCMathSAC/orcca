Explanation A.2.7.33.1.

When we do division with fractions, as with
\(\displaystyle{\frac{4}{15} \div \left(-\frac{5}{9}\right) }\)
we first change division to multiplication, and at the same time, flip the second fraction. In this case, we have
\(\displaystyle{\frac{4}{15} \cdot \left(-\frac{9}{5}\right) }\)
Then we do fraction multiplication as usual. The full process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{4}{15} \div \left(-\frac{5}{9}\right) \amp = \frac{4}{15} \cdot \left(-\frac{9}{5}\right) \amp \hbox{We can divide the first denominator and the second numerator by 3.}\\ \amp = \frac{4}{5 }\cdot\left(-\frac{3}{5}\right) \\ \amp = -\frac{12}{25} \end{aligned} }\)
