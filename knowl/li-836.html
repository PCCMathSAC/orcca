Item 3.10.9.25.a.
A line’s equation in slope-intercept form looks like \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) where \(m\) is the slope, and \(y\) is the \(y\)-coordinate of the \(y\)-intercept. Since the gas is leaking at a rate of \(5.6\) grams per minute, we know that the slope of the linear model is \(-5.6\text{.}\)
So now we have that \(y=-5.6x+b\text{.}\) The next step is to find the value of \(b\text{.}\) There are at least two ways.
One way is to substitute a known point into \(y=-5.6x+b\text{.}\) We use the point \((7,240.8)\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y \amp = -5.6x + b \\ 240.8 \amp = -5.6 \cdot 7 + b \\ 240.8 \amp = -39.2 + b \\ 240.8\mathbf{{} + 39.2} \amp = -39.2 + b\mathbf{{} + 39.2} \\ 280 \amp = b\\ b \amp = 280 \end{aligned}\)
This line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y=-5.6x+280\text{.}\)
Another way is to use the line’s point-slope equation \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\text{.}\) Again, we use the point \((7,240.8)\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y-y_{1} \amp = m(x-x_{1}) \\ y-240.8 \amp = -5.6(x-7) \\ y-240.8 \amp = -5.6x - 5.6 \cdot (-7) \\ y-240.8 \amp = -5.6x+39.2 \\ y-240.8\mathbf{{}+240.8} \amp = -5.6x+39.2\mathbf{{}+240.8} \\ y \amp = -5.6x+280 \end{aligned}\)
This is the second way to find \(b\text{,}\) and thus the line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y=-5.6x+280\text{.}\)
