Explanation 3.2.1.1.
We substitute in each ordered pair’s \(x\)- and \(y\)-values into the equation \(y=9 x+4\text{,}\) and see whether the resulting equation is true.
In two cases, \((-2,-14)\) and \((-5,-41)\text{,}\) we have true equations:
\(\begin{aligned} -14 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 9(-2)+4 \amp -41 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 9(-5)+4\\ -14 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -18+4 \amp -41 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -45+4\\ -14 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -14 \amp -41 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} -41 \end{aligned}\)
So \((-2,-14)\) and \((-5,-41)\) are solutions to the given equation.
In the other two cases, \((4,42)\) and \((0,5)\text{,}\) we have false equations:
\(\begin{aligned} 42 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 9(4)+4 \amp 5 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 9(0)+4\\ 42 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 36+4 \amp 5 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 0+4\\ 42 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 40 \amp 5 \amp \stackrel{?}{=} 4 \end{aligned}\)
So \((4,42)\) and \((0,5)\) are not solutions.
