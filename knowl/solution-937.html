Explanation A.2.7.96.1.

There are a total of \(500\) cups of pudding, with each serving being \({{\frac{1}{10}}}\) cup. To find how many servings can be served, we need to find how many “\({{\frac{1}{10}}}\) cups” are there in \(500\) cups, a division problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}[t] 500 \div {{\frac{1}{10}}} \amp = \frac{500}{1} \div \frac{1}{10} \\ \amp = \frac{500}{1} \cdot \frac{10}{1} \\ \amp = \frac{500\cdot10}{1 \cdot 1} \\ \amp = \frac{5000}{1} \\ \amp = {5000} \end{aligned} }\)
The restaurant can serve \({5000}\) customers at the festival with the \(500\) cups of pudding.
Shortcut
Since each serving has \({{\frac{1}{10}}}\) cup, we know each cup has \(10\) servings. So \(500\) cups has \(500\cdot10={5000}\) servings.
