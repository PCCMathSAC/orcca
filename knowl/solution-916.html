Explanation A.2.7.75.1.

When doing arithmetic with fractions, it helps to rewrite any integers as fractions:
\(\displaystyle{ 1 = \frac{1}{1} }\)
Next, to subtract a fraction, we need to find a common denominator. In this case, it is simply the second denominator \(3\text{.}\) We will change the first fraction to have the common denominator:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{1}{1} \amp = \frac{1 \cdot 3}{1 \cdot 3}\\ \amp = \frac{3}{3} \end{aligned}}\)
Finally, we subtract the numerators and keep the denominator unchanged.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 1 - \frac{23}{3} \amp = \frac{3}{3} - \frac{23}{3} \\ \amp = \frac{3 - 23}{3} \\ \amp = -\frac{20}{3} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer to this question is \(\displaystyle{ -\frac{20}{3}}\text{.}\)
