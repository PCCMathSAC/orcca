Explanation 3.7.5.21.1.
A line’s standard form is \(Ax+By=C\text{.}\) We need to move \(x\) and \(y\) terms to the same side of the equals sign.
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} {y}\amp={\frac{3}{2}x+6}\\ \multiplyleft{2}{y}\amp=\multiplyleft{2}\left({\frac{3}{2}x+6}\right)\\ 2y\amp=2\left( \frac{3}{2}x \right)+2(6)\\ 2y\amp=3x+12\\ 2y\subtractright{3x}\amp=3x+12\subtractright{3x}\\ {-3x+2y}\amp=12\\ \multiplyleft{-1}({-3x+2y})\amp=\multiplyleft{-1}(12)\\ {3x-2y}\amp=-12 \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
Note that \({-3x+2y}=12\) is already in standard form, but it’s better to get rid of the leading negative sign in an equation.
