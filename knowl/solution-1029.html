Explanation A.4.3.24.1.

Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem. Assume \(130\%\) of \(x\) is \(1001\text{,}\) so “\(130\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(1001\) out of \(x\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{130}{100} \amp = \frac{1001}{x} \\ 130x \amp = 100 \cdot 1001 \\ 130x \amp = 100100 \\ \frac{130x}{130} \amp = \frac{100100}{130} \\ x \amp = 770 \end{aligned} }\)
\(130\%\) of \(770\) is \(1001\text{.}\)
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem. This translation from English to math may help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \iff 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
The question is: \(1001\) is \(130\%\) of what? Assume \(1001\) is \(130\%\) of \(x\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} 1001 \amp = 1.3 \cdot x \\ \frac{1001}{1.3} \amp = \frac{1.3x}{1.3} \\ 770 \amp = x \end{aligned} }\)
\(130\%\) of \(770\) is \(1001\text{.}\)
Method 3
In the sentence “\(130\%\) of what is \(1001\text{,}\)”
  • \(1001\) is the percentage,
  • \(130\%\) is the rate,
  • “what” is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}} = \frac{1001}{130\%} = \frac{1001}{1.3} = 770 }\)
\(130\%\) of \(770\) is \(1001\text{.}\)
