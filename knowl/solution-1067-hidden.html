To calculate the percentage increase/decrease, first we find the amount of increase/decrease by doing a simple subtraction calculation, and then we find the percentage increase/decrease.
In this problem, the amount of decrease is \(120-200=80\text{.}\)
Next, since we started with \(200\) cats, we need to ask: \(80\) is what percent of \(200\text{?}\)
Assume \(80\) is \(x\%\) of \(200\text{,}\) so “\(x\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(80\) out of \(200\)”.
Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{x}{100} \amp = \frac{80}{200} \\ 200x \amp = 80 \cdot 100 \\ 200x \amp = 8000 \\ \frac{200x}{200} \amp = \frac{8000}{200} \\ x \amp = 40 \end{aligned} }\)
The percentage decrease of the shelter’s cat population is \(40\%\text{.}\)
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem.
This translation from English to may should help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \Longleftrightarrow 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
Let \(x\) be the unknown percentage, and let the decrease \(80\) be \(x\) (percent) of \(200\text{.}\) That means:
\(\begin{aligned} 80 \amp = x \cdot 200 \\ \frac{80}{200} \amp = \frac{x \cdot 200}{200} \\ 0.4 \amp = x \\ 40\% \amp = x \\ \end{aligned}\)
The percentage decrease of the shelter’s cat population is \(40\%\text{.}\)
Method 3
We first divide the “new number” by the “original number”:
\(\displaystyle{ \frac{120}{200} = 0.6 = 60\% }\)
So the new number is \(60\%\) of the original number, implying the percentage decrease is \(100\% - 60\% = 40\%\text{.}\)
The percentage decrease of the shelter’s cat population is \(40\%\text{.}\)
