This problem can be boiled down to this question: \(3.9\) is \(10\%\) of what? We will show multiple methods to solve this problem.
Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem. Assume \(3.9\) is \(10\%\) of \(x\text{,}\) so “\(10\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(3.9\) out of \(x\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{10}{100} \amp = \frac{3.9}{x} \\ 10x \amp = 100 \cdot 3.9 \\ 10x \amp = 390 \\ \frac{10x}{10} \amp = \frac{390}{10} \\ x \amp = 39 \end{aligned} }\)
The original bill (not including the tip) was \({\$39.00}\text{.}\)
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem. This translation from English to math may help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \iff 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
The question is: \(3.9\) is \(10\%\) of what? Assume \(3.9\) is \(10\%\) of \(x\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} 3.9 \amp = 0.1 \cdot x \\ \frac{3.9}{0.1} \amp = \frac{0.1x}{0.1} \\ 39 \amp = x \end{aligned} }\)
The original bill (not including the tip) was \({\$39.00}\text{.}\)
Method 3
In the sentence “\(3.9\) is \(10\%\) of what,”
  • \(3.9\) is the percentage,
  • \(10\%\) is the rate,
  • “what” is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}} = \frac{3.9}{10\%} = \frac{3.9}{0.1} = 39 }\)
The original bill (not including the tip) was \({\$39.00}\text{.}\)
