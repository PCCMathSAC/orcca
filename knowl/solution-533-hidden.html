When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 18x+15y=4 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 18x+15y \amp = 4 \\ 18x+15y\mathbf{{}-18x} \amp = 4\mathbf{{}-18x} \\ 15y \amp = -18x +4 \\ \frac{15y}{15} \amp = \frac{-18x}{15} + \frac{4}{15}\\ y \amp = -\frac{18}{15}x + \frac{4}{15}\\ y \amp = -\frac{6}{5}x + \frac{4}{15} \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(-\frac{6}{5}\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \(\left(0,\frac{4}{15}\right)\text{.}\)
