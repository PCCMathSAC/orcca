Explanation 3.10.9.29.1.
When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form. In this form, \(m\) is the line’s slope, and \(b\) is the coordinate on the \(y\)-axis where the line intercepts the \(y\)-axis.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given as \(\displaystyle{ -{5}x+y= -8 }\text{.}\) It would be helpful to algebraically rearrange this into slope-intercept form: \(y= mx+b\text{.}\)
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} -{5}x+y \amp = -8 \\ -{5}x+y\mathbf{{}+{5}x} \amp = -8\mathbf{{}+{5}x} \\ y \amp = {5}x - 8 \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \({5}\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \((0,-8)\text{.}\)
