We substitute in each ordered pair’s \(x\)- and \(y\)-values into the equation \(y=\frac{2}{5} x-3\text{,}\) and see whether the resulting equation is true.
In two cases, \((-25,-13)\) and \((25,7)\text{,}\) we have true equations:
\(\begin{aligned} -13 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}\frac{2}{5}(-25)-3 \amp 7 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}\frac{2}{5}(25)-3\\ -13 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-10-3 \amp 7 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}10-3\\ -13 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-13 \amp 7 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}7\\ \end{aligned}\)
So \((-25,-13)\) and \((25,7)\) are solutions to the given equation.
In the other two cases, \((-5,-2)\) and \((0,0)\text{,}\) we have false equations:
\(\begin{aligned} -2 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}\frac{2}{5}(-5)-3 \amp 0 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}\frac{2}{5}(0)-3\\ -2 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-2-3 \amp 0 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}0-3\\ -2 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-5 \amp 0 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-3\\ \end{aligned}\)
So \((-5,-2)\) and \((0,0)\) are not solutions.
