Explanation 3.7.5.19.1.
A line’s standard form is \(Ax+By=C\text{.}\) We need to move \(x\) and \(y\) terms to the same side of the equals sign.
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} {y}\amp={9x+6}\\ {y}\subtractright{9x}\amp={9x+6}\subtractright{9x}\\ {-9x+y}\amp=6\\ (-1)({-9x+y})\amp=(-1)(6)\\ {9x-y}\amp=-6 \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
Note that \({-9x+y}=6\) is already in standard form, but it’s better to get rid of the leading negative sign in an equation.
