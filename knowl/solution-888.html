Explanation A.2.7.47.1.

To add two fractions with different denominators, we first have to find a common denominator.
In this case, the least common multiple of \(7\) and \(6\) is \(42\text{.}\)
Look at the first fraction \(\displaystyle{\frac{3}{7}}\text{.}\) To change the denominator to \(42\text{,}\) we multiply \(6\) at both the top and bottom, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{3}{7} \amp = \frac{3 \cdot 6}{7 \cdot 6}\\ \amp = \frac{18}{42}\end{aligned} }\)
Similarly, we change the second fraction’s denominator to \(42\text{:}\)
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{1}{6} \amp = \frac{1 \cdot 7}{6 \cdot 7}\\ \amp = \frac{7}{42} \end{aligned}}\)
Now we can add those two fractions. The full process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \frac{3}{7} + \frac{1}{6} \amp = \frac{18}{42} + \frac{7}{42} \\ \amp = \frac{18 + 7}{42} \\ \amp = \frac{25}{42} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer is \(\displaystyle{\frac{25}{42}}\text{.}\)
