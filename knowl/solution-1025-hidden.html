Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem. Assume \(51\%\) of \(x\) is \(193.8\text{,}\) so “\(51\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(193.8\) out of \(x\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{51}{100} \amp = \frac{193.8}{x} \\ 51x \amp = 100 \cdot 193.8 \\ 51x \amp = 19380 \\ \frac{51x}{51} \amp = \frac{19380}{51} \\ x \amp = 380 \end{aligned} }\)
\(51\%\) of \(380\) is \(193.8\text{.}\)
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem. This translation from English to math may help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \iff 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
The question is: \(193.8\) is \(51\%\) of what? Assume \(193.8\) is \(51\%\) of \(x\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} 193.8 \amp = 0.51 \cdot x \\ \frac{193.8}{0.51} \amp = \frac{0.51x}{0.51} \\ 380 \amp = x \end{aligned} }\)
\(51\%\) of \(380\) is \(193.8\text{.}\)
Method 3
In the sentence “\(51\%\) of what is \(193.8\text{,}\)”
  • \(193.8\) is the percentage,
  • \(51\%\) is the rate,
  • “what” is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}} = \frac{193.8}{51\%} = \frac{193.8}{0.51} = 380 }\)
\(51\%\) of \(380\) is \(193.8\text{.}\)
