When doing arithmetic with fractions, it is helpful to rewrite any integers as fractions:
\(\displaystyle{ 4 = \frac{4}{1} }\)
Next, to add two fractions, we need to find a common denominator. In this case, it is simply the second denominator \(5\text{.}\) We will rewrite the first fraction:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{4}{1} \amp = \frac{4 \cdot 5}{1 \cdot 5}\\ \amp = \frac{20}{5} \end{aligned}}\)
Finally, we add the numerators and keep the denominator unchanged. The whole process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 4 + \frac{3}{5} \amp = \frac{20}{5} + \frac{3}{5} \\ \amp = \frac{20 + 3}{5} \\ \amp = \frac{23}{5} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer to this question is \({{\frac{23}{5}}}\text{.}\)
