Explanation 3.2.23.1.
Since this equation has a fractional coefficient for \(x\) with denominator \(8\text{,}\) it would be wise to choose our own \(x\)-values that are multiples of \(8\text{.}\) Then when we use them to solve for \(y\text{,}\) the denominator will be cleared, and we will not need to continue with fraction arithmetic.
This solution will use the \(x\)-values \(-8\text{,}\) \(0\text{,}\) \(8\text{,}\) \(16\) and \(24\text{.}\) The choice to use these \(x\)-values is arbitrary, but they are small multiples of \(8\text{,}\) which will make computation easier.
One at a time, we substitute these \(x\)-values into the equation \(y={\frac{5}{8}}x - 5\text{,}\) and solve for \(y\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} y = {\frac{5}{8}}\left( -8 \right) - 5 \amp \implies y=-10\\ y = {\frac{5}{8}}\left( 0 \right) - 5 \amp \implies y=-5\\ y = {\frac{5}{8}}\left( 8 \right) - 5 \amp \implies y=0\\ y = {\frac{5}{8}}\left( 16 \right) - 5 \amp \implies y=5\\ y = {\frac{5}{8}}\left( 24 \right) - 5 \amp \implies y=10\\ \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
