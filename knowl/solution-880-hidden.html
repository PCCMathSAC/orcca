In order to multiply two mixed numbers, like
\(\displaystyle{ {1 {\textstyle\frac{7}{8}}} \cdot {1 {\textstyle\frac{3}{25}}} }\)
it helps to first change both mixed numbers to improper fractions. In this case, the problem becomes
\(\displaystyle{\frac{15}{8} \cdot \frac{28}{25}}\)
Then we do fraction multiplication as usual. To avoid dealing with big numbers, we reduce fractions before doing multiplications. The full solution is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] {1 {\textstyle\frac{7}{8}}} \cdot {1 {\textstyle\frac{3}{25}}} \amp = \frac{15}{8} \cdot \frac{28}{25} \\ \amp = \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{5} \\ \amp = \frac{21}{10} \end{aligned} }\)
If we like, we may convert this answer to a mixed number since the original factors were mixed numbers. The solution is \({2 {\textstyle\frac{1}{10}}}\text{.}\)
