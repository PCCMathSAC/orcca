Item 3.5.7.68.a.
A line’s equation in slope-intercept form looks like \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) where \(m\) is the slope, and \(y\) is the \(y\)-coordinate of the \(y\)-intercept. Since the gas is leaking at a rate of \(1.4\) grams per minute, we know that the slope of the linear model is \(-1.4\text{.}\)
So now we have that \(y=-1.4x+b\text{.}\) The next step is to find the value of \(b\text{.}\) There are at least two ways.
One way is to substitute a known point into \(y=-1.4x+b\text{.}\) We use the point \((9,47.6)\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y \amp = -1.4x + b \\ 47.6 \amp = -1.4 \cdot 9 + b \\ 47.6 \amp = -12.6 + b \\ 47.6\mathbf{{} + 12.6} \amp = -12.6 + b\mathbf{{} + 12.6} \\ 60.2 \amp = b\\ b \amp = 60.2 \end{aligned}\)
This line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y=-1.4x+60.2\text{.}\)
Another way is to use the line’s point-slope equation \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\text{.}\) Again, we use the point \((9,47.6)\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y-y_{1} \amp = m(x-x_{1}) \\ y-47.6 \amp = -1.4(x-9) \\ y-47.6 \amp = -1.4x - 1.4 \cdot (-9) \\ y-47.6 \amp = -1.4x+12.6 \\ y-47.6\mathbf{{}+47.6} \amp = -1.4x+12.6\mathbf{{}+47.6} \\ y \amp = -1.4x+60.2 \end{aligned}\)
This is the second way to find \(b\text{,}\) and thus the line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y=-1.4x+60.2\text{.}\)
