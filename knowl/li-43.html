Item 1.3.4.75.d.
Notice that \(x + 2y\) and \(2x + y\) both have an \(x\) and a \(y\text{.}\) Let’s subtract an \(x\) and a \(y\) from those expressions.
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} x + 2y \amp \wonder{\gt} 2x + y\\ x + 2y - x - y \amp \wonder{\gt} 2x + y - x - y\\ y \amp \wonder{\gt} x \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
Since \(y \gt x\text{,}\) this means that \(x + 2y \gt 2x + y\text{.}\)
