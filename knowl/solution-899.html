Explanation A.2.7.58.1.

To add two fractions with different denominators, we first have to find a common denominator.
In this case, the least common multiple of \(9\) and \(10\) is \(90\text{.}\)
Look at the first fraction \(\displaystyle{-\frac{1}{9}}\text{.}\) To change the denominator to \(90\text{,}\) we multiply \(10\) at both the top and bottom, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} -\frac{1}{9} \amp = -\frac{1 \cdot 10}{9 \cdot 10}\\ \amp = -\frac{10}{90} \end{aligned}}\)
Similarly, we change the second fraction’s denominator to \(90\text{:}\)
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{1}{10} \amp = \frac{1 \cdot 9}{10 \cdot 9}\\ \amp = \frac{9}{90} \end{aligned}}\)
Now we can add those two fractions. The full process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} -\frac{1}{9} + \frac{1}{10} \amp = -\frac{10}{90} + \frac{9}{90} \\ \amp = \frac{-10 + 9}{90} \\ \amp = -\frac{1}{90} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer to this question is \(\displaystyle{-\frac{1}{90}}\text{.}\)
