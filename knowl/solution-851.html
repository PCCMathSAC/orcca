Explanation A.2.7.10.1.

Method 1:
We find a number that divides into both the numerator \(20\text{,}\) and the denominator \(39\text{.}\)
Check the first few prime numbers one by one: \(2, 3, 5, 7, \ldots\text{,}\) until the prime number is greater than or equal to \(20\text{.}\) No prime number goes into both \(20\) and \(39\text{,}\) so the fraction \({{\frac{20}{39}}}\) is not reducible.
Method 2:
A second method to reduce fraction is to prime factor both the numerator and the denominator, and then cancel out factors in pairs: one from the numerator and one from the denominator.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{20}{39} \amp = \frac{2 \cdot 2 \cdot 5}{3 \cdot 13} \end{aligned} }\)
Notice that no numbers can be canceled out, so the fraction \({{\frac{20}{39}}}\) is not reducible.
