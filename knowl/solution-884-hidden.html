To add two fractions with different denominators, we first have to find a common denominator.
In this case, the least common multiple of \(7\) and \(35\) is \(35\text{.}\)
Look at the first fraction \(\displaystyle{\frac{1}{7}}\text{.}\) To change the denominator to \(35\text{,}\) we multiply \(5\) at both the top and bottom, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{7} \amp = \frac{1 \cdot 5}{7 \cdot 5}\\ \amp = \frac{5}{35} \end{aligned}}\)
The second fraction already has the common denominator.
Finally, we can do the addition. The whole process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{7} + \frac{22}{35} \amp = \frac{5}{35} + \frac{22}{35} \\ \amp = \frac{5 + 22}{35} \\ \amp = \frac{27}{35} \end{aligned} }\)
The answer is \(\displaystyle{\frac{27}{35}}\text{.}\)
