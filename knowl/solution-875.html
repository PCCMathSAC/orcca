Explanation A.2.7.34.1.

When we do division with fractions, as with
\(\displaystyle{\frac{1}{10} \div \left(-\frac{8}{15}\right) }\)
we first change division to multiplication, and at the same time, flip the second fraction. In this case, we have
\(\displaystyle{\frac{1}{10} \cdot \left(-\frac{15}{8}\right) }\)
Then we do fraction multiplication as usual. The full process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{1}{10} \div \left(-\frac{8}{15}\right) \amp = \frac{1}{10} \cdot \left(-\frac{15}{8}\right) \amp \hbox{We can divide the first denominator and the second numerator by 5.}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{2 }\cdot\left(-\frac{3}{8}\right) \\ \amp = -\frac{3}{16} \end{aligned} }\)
