When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 5x-6y=-18 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 5x-6y \amp = -18 \\ 5x-6y\mathbf{{}-5x} \amp = -18\mathbf{{}-5x} \\ -6y \amp = -5x - 18 \\ \frac{-6y}{-6} \amp = \frac{-5x}{-6} + \frac{-18}{-6} \\ y \amp = \frac{5}{6}x + 3 \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(\displaystyle{ \frac{5}{6} }\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \({\left(0,3\right)}\text{.}\)
