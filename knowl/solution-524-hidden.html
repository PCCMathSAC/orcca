When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 10x+5y=15 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 10x+5y \amp = 15 \\ 10x+5y\mathbf{{} -10x} \amp = 15\mathbf{{} -10x} \\ 5y \amp = -10x+15 \\ \frac{5y}{5} \amp = \frac{-10x}{5} + \frac{15}{5} \\ y \amp = {-2}x + 3 \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \({-2}\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \({\left(0,3\right)}\text{.}\)
