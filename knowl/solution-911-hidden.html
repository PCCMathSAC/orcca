To subtract two fractions with different denominators, we first have to find a common denominator.
In this case, the least common multiple of \(45\) and \(9\) is \(45\text{.}\)
The first fraction already has the common denominator.
Look at the second fraction \(\displaystyle{\frac{4}{9}}\text{.}\) To change the denominator to \(45\text{,}\) we multiply \(5\) at both the top and bottom, and we have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\frac{4}{9} \amp = \frac{4 \cdot 5}{9 \cdot 5}\\ \amp = \frac{20}{45} \end{aligned}}\)
Finally, we can do the subtraction. The whole process is:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \frac{44}{45} - \frac{4}{9} \amp = \frac{44}{45} - \frac{20}{45} \\ \amp = \frac{44 - 20}{45} \\ \amp = \frac{24}{45} \amp \hbox{We can reduce this by dividing 3 into both the numerator and the denominator. }\\ \amp = \frac{8}{15} \end{aligned} }\)
