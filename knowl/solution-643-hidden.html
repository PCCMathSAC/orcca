When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 15x+6y=1 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 15x+6y \amp = 1 \\ 15x+6y\mathbf{{}-15x} \amp = 1\mathbf{{}-15x} \\ 6y \amp = -15x +1 \\ \frac{6y}{6} \amp = \frac{-15x}{6} + \frac{1}{6}\\ y \amp = -\frac{15}{6}x + \frac{1}{6}\\ y \amp = -\frac{5}{2}x + \frac{1}{6} \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(-\frac{5}{2}\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \(\left(0,\frac{1}{6}\right)\text{.}\)
