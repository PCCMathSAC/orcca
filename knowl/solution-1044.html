Explanation A.4.3.39.1.

This problem can be boiled down to this question: \(215\) is what percent of \(430\text{?}\) We will show multiple methods to solve this problem.
Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem.
Assume \(215\) is \(x\%\) of \(430\text{,}\) so “\(x\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(215\) out of \(430\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{x}{100} \amp = \frac{215}{430} \\ 430x \amp = 215 \cdot 100 \\ 430x \amp = 21500 \\ \frac{430x}{430} \amp = \frac{21500}{430} \\ x \amp = 50 \end{aligned} }\)
Kimball made \(50\%\) of free throws in the past few seasons.
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem.
This translation from English to may should help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \Longleftrightarrow 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
Assume \(215\) is \(x\) (as a percent) of \(430\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 215 \amp = x \cdot 430 \\ \frac{215}{430} \amp = \frac{x \cdot 430}{430} \\ 0.5 \amp = x\\ x \amp = 50\% \end{aligned} }\)
Kimball made \(50\%\) of free throws in the past few seasons.
Method 3
In the sentence “\(215\) is what percent of \(430\text{,}\)”
  • \(215\) is the percentage,
  • “what percent” is the rate,
  • \(430\) is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{rate} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{base}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{rate} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{base}} = \frac{215}{430} = 0.5 = 50\% }\)
Kimball made \(50\%\) of free throws in the past few seasons.
