Explanation 3.8.5.32.1.
To find a line’s slope, we can use the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{slope}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}} }\)
For line \(m\text{,}\) we have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\text{slope}\amp =\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\\ \amp =\frac{-5-3}{15-(-5)}\\ \amp =\frac{-8}{20}\\ \amp =-\frac{2}{5} \end{aligned}}\)
For line \(n\text{,}\) we have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\text{slope}\amp =\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\\ \amp =\frac{-4-2}{-5-(-20)}\\ \amp =\frac{-6}{15}\\ \amp =-\frac{2}{5} \end{aligned}}\)
Since these two lines have the same slope, they are parallel to each other.
