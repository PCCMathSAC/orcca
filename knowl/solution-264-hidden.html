We substitute in each ordered pair’s \(x\)- and \(y\)-values into the equation \(y=-\frac{5}{6} x-4\text{,}\) and see whether the resulting equation is true.
In two cases, \((-30,21)\) and \((0,-4)\text{,}\) we have true equations:
\(\begin{aligned} 21 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{5}{6}(-30)-4 \amp -4 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{5}{6}(0)-4\\ 21 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}25-4 \amp -4 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}0-4\\ 21 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}21 \amp -4 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-4\\ \end{aligned}\)
So \((-30,21)\) and \((0,-4)\) are solutions to the given equation.
In the other two cases, \((30,-28)\) and \((-6,6)\text{,}\) we have false equations:
\(\begin{aligned} 6 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{5}{6}(-6)-4 \amp -28 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-\frac{5}{6}(30)-4\\ 6 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}5-4 \amp -28 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-25-4\\ 6 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}1 \amp -28 \amp \stackrel{?}{=}-29\\ \end{aligned}\)
So \((30,-28)\) and \((-6,6)\) are not solutions.
