The slope-intercept form of a line’s equation has the form \(y=mx+b\) where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the \(y\)-coordinate of the \(y\)-intercept. To find a line’s slope-intercept equation, we first try to find the line’s slope.
To find a line’s slope, we can use the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{slope}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}} }\)
First, we mark which number corresponds to which variable in the formula:
\(\displaystyle{ (18,{1}) \longrightarrow (x_{1},y_{1}) }\)
\(\displaystyle{ (9,{3}) \longrightarrow (x_{2},y_{2}) }\)
Now we substitute these numbers into the corresponding variables in the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\text{slope}\amp =\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\\ \amp =\frac{{3}-{1}}{9-18}\\ \amp =\frac{{2}}{-9}\\ \amp ={-{\frac{2}{9}}} \end{aligned}}\)
Now we have \(y={-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+b\text{.}\) The next step is to find the value of \(b\text{.}\) There are at least two ways we can do this.
One way is to substitute one of the given points into \(y={-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+b\text{.}\) We choose to use \((18,{1})\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y \amp = {-{\frac{2}{9}}}x + b \\ {1} \amp = {-{\frac{2}{9}}} \cdot 18 + b \\ {1} \amp = {-4} + b \\ {1}\mathbf{{}-{-4}} \amp = {-4} + b\mathbf{{}-{-4}} \\ 5 \amp = b\\ b \amp = 5 \end{aligned}\)
This line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y={-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+5\text{.}\)
Another way is to use the line’s point-slope equation \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\text{.}\) Again, we choose to use the point \((18,{1})\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y-y_{1} \amp = m(x-x_{1}) \\ y-{1} \amp = {-{\frac{2}{9}}}(x-18) \\ y-{1} \amp = {-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+{-{\frac{2}{9}}} \cdot (-18) \\ y-{1} \amp = {-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+4 \\ y-{1}\mathbf{{}+{1}} \amp = {-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+4\mathbf{{}+{1}} \\ y \amp = {-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+5 \end{aligned}\)
This is the second way to find \(b\text{,}\) and thus the line’s slope-intercept equation is \(\displaystyle{ y={-{\frac{2}{9}}}x+5 }\text{.}\)
