  1. A line’s equation in point-slope form looks like \(y=m(x-x_0)+y_0\text{,}\) where \(m\) is the slope, and \((x_0,y_0)\) is some point we know to be on the line. We first need to find the line’s slope. Since the gas is leaking at a rate of \(5.4\) grams per minute, we know that the slope of the linear model is \(-5.4\text{.}\)
    So now we have that \(y=-5.4(x-x_0)+y_0\text{.}\) We can use \((x_0,y_0)=(8,183.6)\) to get \({y = -5.4\mathopen{}\left(x-8\right)+183.6}\text{.}\)
  2. After \(39\) minutes, we can find the mass of the remaining gas if we substitute \(39\) in for \(x\) in the equation \(y=-5.4(x-8)+183.6\text{,}\) and we have:
    \(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} y \amp = -5.4(39-8) +183.6 \\ y \amp = 16.2 \end{aligned} }\)
    So after \(39\) minutes, there are \(16.2\) grams of gas remaining.
  3. To find when the gas will be all gone, we need to find when there are \(0\) grams of gas left. We can substitute \(0\) in for \(y\) in the equation \(y=-5.4(x-8)+183.6\text{,}\) and we have:
    \(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} y \amp = -5.4(x-8) +183.6 \\ 0 \amp = -5.4(x-8) +183.6 \\ -183.6 \amp = -5.4(x-8)\\ \frac{-183.6}{-5.4} \amp = x - 8\\ 34 \amp = x - 8\\ 42 \amp = x\\ 42 \amp = x \end{aligned} }\)
    So after \(42\) minutes, the gas will be all gone.
