Explanation 3.5.7.55.1.
The slope-intercept form of a line’s equation has the form \(y=mx+b\) where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the \(y\)-coordinate of the \(y\)-intercept. To find a line’s slope-intercept equation, we first try to find the line’s slope.
To find a line’s slope, we can use the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{slope}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}} }\)
First, we mark which number corresponds to which variable in the formula:
\(\displaystyle{ (-9,{-15}) \longrightarrow (x_{1},y_{1}) }\)
\(\displaystyle{ (3,{17}) \longrightarrow (x_{2},y_{2}) }\)
Now we substitute these numbers into the corresponding variables in the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\text{slope}\amp =\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\\ \amp =\frac{{17}-((-15))}{3-(-9)}\\ \amp =\frac{{32}}{12}\\ \amp ={{\frac{8}{3}}} \end{aligned}}\)
Now we have \(y={\frac{8}{3}x}+b\text{.}\) The next step is to find the value of \(b\text{.}\) There are at least two ways we can do this.
One way is to substitute one of the given points into \(y={\frac{8}{3}x}+b\text{.}\) We choose to use \((-9,{-15})\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y \amp = {\frac{8}{3}x} + b \\ {-15} \amp = {{\frac{8}{3}}} \cdot -9 + b \\ {-15} \amp = {-24} + b \\ {-15}\mathbf{{}-{-24}} \amp = {-24} + b\mathbf{{}-{-24}} \\ 9 \amp = b\\ b \amp = 9 \end{aligned}\)
This line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y={\frac{8}{3}x}+9\text{.}\)
Another way is to use the line’s point-slope equation \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\text{.}\) Again, we choose to use the point \((-9,{-15})\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y-y_{1} \amp = m(x-x_{1}) \\ y-{-15} \amp = {{\frac{8}{3}}}(x + 9) \\ y-{-15} \amp = {\frac{8}{3}x}+{{\frac{8}{3}}} \cdot ( + 9) \\ y-{-15} \amp = {\frac{8}{3}x}+24 \\ y-{-15}\mathbf{{}+{-15}} \amp = {\frac{8}{3}x}+24\mathbf{{}+{-15}} \\ y \amp = {\frac{8}{3}x}+9 \end{aligned}\)
This is the second way to find \(b\text{,}\) and thus the line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y={{\frac{8}{3}}}x+9\text{.}\)
