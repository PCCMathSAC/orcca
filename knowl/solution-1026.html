Explanation A.4.3.21.1.

Method 1
We will use proportion to solve this problem. Assume \(2\%\) of \(x\) is \(9.6\text{,}\) so “\(2\) out of \(100\)” corresponds to “\(9.6\) out of \(x\)”.
We will write and solve the proportion:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned}[t] \frac{2}{100} \amp = \frac{9.6}{x} \\ 2x \amp = 100 \cdot 9.6 \\ 2x \amp = 960 \\ \frac{2x}{2} \amp = \frac{960}{2} \\ x \amp = 480 \end{aligned} }\)
\(2\%\) of \(480\) is \(9.6\text{.}\)
Method 2
We will use the percentage formula to solve this problem. This translation from English to math may help you remember the percentage formula.
\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4 \iff 2 = 0.5 \cdot 4\)
The question is: \(9.6\) is \(2\%\) of what? Assume \(9.6\) is \(2\%\) of \(x\text{.}\) We have:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} 9.6 \amp = 0.02 \cdot x \\ \frac{9.6}{0.02} \amp = \frac{0.02x}{0.02} \\ 480 \amp = x \end{aligned} }\)
\(2\%\) of \(480\) is \(9.6\text{.}\)
Method 3
In the sentence “\(2 \text{ is } 50\% \text{ of } 4\text{,}\)”
  • \(2\) is the percentage,
  • \(50\%\) is the rate,
  • \(4\) is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{base} = \frac{9.6}{2\%} = \frac{9.6}{0.02} = 480 }\)
\(2\%\) of \(480\) is \(9.6\text{.}\)
Method 3
In the sentence “\(2\%\) of what is \(9.6\text{,}\)”
  • \(9.6\) is the percentage,
  • \(2\%\) is the rate,
  • “what” is the base (following the word “of”).
By the formula \(\text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}}\text{,}\) we do a division to solve the problem:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{base} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{rate}} = \frac{9.6}{2\%} = \frac{9.6}{0.02} = 480 }\)
\(2\%\) of \(480\) is \(9.6\text{.}\)
