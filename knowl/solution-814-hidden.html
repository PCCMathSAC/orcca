There is no operation symbol between \(4.2\) and \(-0.63\text{,}\) which implies multiplication.
Next, since “positive times negative is negative,” we know the answer must be negative. Now we can focus on calculating \(4.2 \cdot 0.63\text{,}\) and in the end add back the negative symbol.
It’s given that \(42\cdot63=2646\text{.}\) We need to calculate \(4.2 \cdot 0.63\text{.}\)
Notice that \(4.2=\frac{42}{10}\) and \(0.63=\frac{63}{100}\text{,}\) so we can calculate \(4.2 \cdot 0.63\) in this way:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}[t] \amp \phantom{{}=} 4.2 \cdot 0.63 \\ \amp = \frac{42}{10} \cdot \frac{63}{100} \\ \amp = \frac{42\cdot63}{10\cdot100} \\ \amp = \frac{2646}{1000} \\ \amp = 2.646 \end{aligned} }\)
Once we understand the math, we can use this shortcut.
  1. To change \(42\) to \(4.2\text{,}\) we moved the decimal point to the left by \(1\) place(s).
  2. To change \(63\) to \(0.63\text{,}\) we moved the decimal point to the left by \(2\) place(s).
  3. We move the decimal point of \(2646\) to the left by \(1+2=3\) places to get \(4.2\cdot0.63=2.646\text{.}\)
Again, don’t forget to add back the negative symbol.
\(4.2(-0.63)=-2.646\)
