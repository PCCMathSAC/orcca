Explanation 3.7.5.44.1.
Assume Virginia will purchase \(x\) tea bags and \(y\) sugar bags.
Since each tea bag costs \(10\) cents, \(x\) tea bags would cost \(10 x\) cents.
Similarly, \(y\) sugar bags would cost \(5 y\) cents.
Since Virginia can spend a total of \({\$3.00}\text{,}\) or \(300\) cents, we can write the equation:
\(\displaystyle{ 10x+5y=300 }\)
A line’s \(y\)-intercept looks like \((0,y)\text{.}\) To find its \(y\) intercept, we substitute \(x\) in the equation with \(0\text{,}\) and we have:
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 10x+5y \amp = 300 \\ 10(0)+5y \amp = 300 \\ 5y \amp = 300 \\ \frac{5x}{5} \amp = \frac{300}{5} \\ x \amp = 60 \end{aligned} }\)
The line’s \(y\)-intercept is \((0,60)\text{.}\)
The correct solution is: The y-intercept is (0, 60). It implies Virginia can purchase 60 sugar bags with no tea bags.
