Explanation 3.7.5.10.1.
When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 25x-5y=-15 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 25x-5y \amp = -15 \\ 25x-5y\mathbf{{}-25x} \amp = -15\mathbf{{}-25x} \\ -5y \amp = -25x - 15 \\ \frac{-5y}{-5} \amp = \frac{-25x}{-5} + \frac{-15}{-5} \\ y \amp = {5}x + 3 \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \({5}\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \({\left(0,3\right)}\text{.}\)
