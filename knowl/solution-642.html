Explanation 3.10.9.31.1.
When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 12x+9y=4 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 12x+9y \amp = 4 \\ 12x+9y\mathbf{{}-12x} \amp = 4\mathbf{{}-12x} \\ 9y \amp = -12x +4 \\ \frac{9y}{9} \amp = \frac{-12x}{9} + \frac{4}{9}\\ y \amp = -\frac{12}{9}x + \frac{4}{9}\\ y \amp = -\frac{4}{3}x + \frac{4}{9} \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(-\frac{4}{3}\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \(\left(0,\frac{4}{9}\right)\text{.}\)
