Explanation 3.5.7.56.1.
The slope-intercept form of a line’s equation has the form \(y=mx+b\) where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the \(y\)-coordinate of the \(y\)-intercept. To find a line’s slope-intercept equation, we first try to find the line’s slope.
To find a line’s slope, we can use the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \text{slope}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}} }\)
First, we mark which number corresponds to which variable in the formula:
\(\displaystyle{ (14,{27}) \longrightarrow (x_{1},y_{1}) }\)
\(\displaystyle{ (7,{18}) \longrightarrow (x_{2},y_{2}) }\)
Now we substitute these numbers into the corresponding variables in the slope formula:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}\text{slope}\amp =\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\\ \amp =\frac{{18}-{27}}{7-14}\\ \amp =\frac{{-9}}{-7}\\ \amp ={{\frac{9}{7}}} \end{aligned}}\)
Now we have \(y={\frac{9}{7}x}+b\text{.}\) The next step is to find the value of \(b\text{.}\) There are at least two ways we can do this.
One way is to substitute one of the given points into \(y={\frac{9}{7}x}+b\text{.}\) We choose to use \((14,{27})\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y \amp = {\frac{9}{7}x} + b \\ {27} \amp = {{\frac{9}{7}}} \cdot 14 + b \\ {27} \amp = {18} + b \\ {27}\mathbf{{}-{18}} \amp = {18} + b\mathbf{{}-{18}} \\ 9 \amp = b\\ b \amp = 9 \end{aligned}\)
This line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y={\frac{9}{7}x}+9\text{.}\)
Another way is to use the line’s point-slope equation \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\text{.}\) Again, we choose to use the point \((14,{27})\text{.}\)
\(\begin{aligned} y-y_{1} \amp = m(x-x_{1}) \\ y-{27} \amp = {{\frac{9}{7}}}(x-14) \\ y-{27} \amp = {\frac{9}{7}x}+{{\frac{9}{7}}} \cdot (-14) \\ y-{27} \amp = {\frac{9}{7}x}-18 \\ y-{27}\mathbf{{}+{27}} \amp = {\frac{9}{7}x}-18\mathbf{{}+{27}} \\ y \amp = {\frac{9}{7}x}+9 \end{aligned}\)
This is the second way to find \(b\text{,}\) and thus the line’s slope-intercept equation is \(y={{\frac{9}{7}}}x+9\text{.}\)
