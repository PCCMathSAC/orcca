Explanation A.1.7.50.1.

There is no operation symbol between \(5.8\) and \(-0.019\text{,}\) which implies multiplication.
Next, since “positive times negative is negative,” we know the answer must be negative. Now we can focus on calculating \(5.8 \cdot 0.019\text{,}\) and in the end add back the negative symbol.
It’s given that \(58\cdot19=1102\text{.}\) We need to calculate \(5.8 \cdot 0.019\text{.}\)
Notice that \(5.8=\frac{58}{10}\) and \(0.019=\frac{19}{1000}\text{,}\) so we can calculate \(5.8 \cdot 0.019\) in this way:
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned}[t] \amp \phantom{{}=} 5.8 \cdot 0.019 \\ \amp = \frac{58}{10} \cdot \frac{19}{1000} \\ \amp = \frac{58\cdot19}{10\cdot1000} \\ \amp = \frac{1102}{10000} \\ \amp = 0.1102 \end{aligned} }\)
Once we understand the math, we can use this shortcut.
  1. To change \(58\) to \(5.8\text{,}\) we moved the decimal point to the left by \(1\) place(s).
  2. To change \(19\) to \(0.019\text{,}\) we moved the decimal point to the left by \(3\) place(s).
  3. We move the decimal point of \(1102\) to the left by \(1+3=4\) places to get \(5.8\cdot0.019=0.1102\text{.}\)
Again, don’t forget to add back the negative symbol.
\(5.8(-0.019)=-0.1102\)
