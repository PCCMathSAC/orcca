When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 5x-4y=-20 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 5x-4y \amp = -20 \\ 5x-4y\mathbf{{}-5x} \amp = -20\mathbf{{}-5x} \\ -4y \amp = -5x - 20 \\ \frac{-4y}{-4} \amp = \frac{-5x}{-4} + \frac{-20}{-4} \\ y \amp = \frac{5}{4}x + 5 \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(\displaystyle{ \frac{5}{4} }\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \({\left(0,5\right)}\text{.}\)
