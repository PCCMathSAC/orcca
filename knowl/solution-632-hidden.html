When an equation of a line is written in the form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it is said to be in slope-intercept form.
In this problem, the line’s equation is given in the standard form \(\displaystyle{ 8x-5y=20 }\text{.}\) If we algebraically rearrange the equation from standard form to slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\text{,}\) it will be easy to see the slope and \(y\)-intercept.
\(\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} 8x-5y \amp = 20 \\ 8x-5y\mathbf{{}-8x} \amp = 20\mathbf{{}-8x} \\ -5y \amp = -8x+20 \\ \frac{-5y}{-5} \amp = \frac{-8x}{-5} + \frac{20}{-5} \\ y \amp = \frac{8}{5}x - 4 \end{aligned} }\)
Now we can see the line’s slope is \(\displaystyle{ \frac{8}{5} }\text{,}\) and its \(y\)-intercept has coordinates \({\left(0,-4\right)}\text{.}\)
